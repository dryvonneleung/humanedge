What is your edge?

Most strengths tests sort people into job types. This one starts somewhere broader.

How do you naturally create value in the world?

Twelve domains across four pillars — what you notice, what you understand, what you create, and how you act. A consultant, an athlete, a nurse, a gamer, a singer, a researcher and a quiet craftsperson should all be able to find themselves in the result, because value gets created in all of those ways.

The assessment measures two things separately: what you are good at, and what energises you. Those are not the same, and the gap between them usually explains more about someone's career than either one alone.

  • Three parts, about 10–12 minutes. 36 rating items, 12 energy ratings, and 4 short reflection questions.
  • Think about how you naturally behave most of the time, across your whole life rather than just your current job. Your instinct is usually more accurate than a considered answer.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. Everything runs in your browser and is saved only on this device. You can download your own results at the end.
  • AI is in the interpretation, not the framework. The domains are about human value creation; where AI helps or doesn't is discussed once you have your profile.

Tip: on the rating screens you can press 1–5 to answer and move to the next item.

Part 1 of 3 · Strengths

Rate how well each statement describes you. The statements are deliberately mixed up rather than grouped by theme, so answer each one on its own terms.

There are no good or bad answers here. Overrating yourself just produces a flatter, less useful profile.

Part 2 of 3 · Energy

Now the other half of the picture. For each kind of activity, how does it feel to do it — regardless of how good you are at it?

Someone can be excellent at coordinating people and quietly hate it. Someone else is equally good and loves it. Those two people need completely different advice, and this is the part that tells them apart.

Part 3 of 3 · Hidden strengths

These aren't scored. They often surface the strengths a rating scale misses — the ones you've stopped noticing because they come easily.

They appear in your report as written, next to your scored profile. Skip any you'd rather not answer.
